Panelists Talent acquisition Management
Pain points
1
Panel ratings don't predict post-hire performance
Management rates 4 of 5 new hires below the panel's original communication score. Panelists are confident (4.1/5) but their signal doesn't translate to on-the-job reality.
2
No post-hire feedback loop — blind spot in the data
Only 14% of TA respondents confirm post-hire communication feedback is gathered. The panel has no signal to self-correct over time.
3
Inconsistent rating standards across panelists
TA rates panelist consistency at only 67% effective. Even supportive panelists flag that "what constitutes a 7 vs 8" is not calibrated.
Recommended actions
A
Introduce calibration sessions quarterly
Run panelist calibration sessions where the same candidate profile is rated by multiple panelists independently, then discussed. Address "7 vs 8" ambiguity with worked examples.
B
Create a post-hire feedback mechanism
At 60 and 90 days post-joining, line managers complete a 3-question communication assessment. Feed results back to the panel who interviewed the candidate.
C
Publish a communication rubric
Define what "clarity", "listening", and "structure" mean at each score band (1–3, 4–6, 7–8, 9–10) with observable behaviours. Share with all panelists before next interview cycle.

Panelists TA Management
Pain points
1
Back-to-back interviews are the #1 delay driver
Repeatedly cited by panelists: no buffer time after interviews leads to delayed or incomplete submissions. Takes 15–30 mins per feedback — unplanned time blocks cause slippage.
2
Current template is "functional but rigid"
70% rate the template as effective, but the most common qualifier is "could benefit from an optional narrative section." Structured but lacks nuance.
3
Feedback tracking is fragmented across email + system
TA respondents describe feedback as "stored in recruitment system AND shared by email" — no single source of truth. Manual follow-ups required.
Recommended actions
A
Enforce a 15-minute buffer after every interview
Calendar scheduling rules should prevent back-to-back bookings. A 15-minute block post-interview enables immediate feedback while recall is fresh — addressing the #1 cited barrier.
B
Add an optional narrative summary field to the template
76% of panelists want better tooling. Adding one free-text "overall impression" field would address the "rigid" feedback without restructuring the whole form.
C
Consolidate feedback into a single tracked system
Eliminate email as a feedback channel. All submissions go through the recruitment system with timestamp and completion-status visible to TA in real time.

Panelists TA Human capital
Pain points
1
Load distribution is unanimously seen as unfair
0% of panelists (in the fairness question) say workload is evenly distributed. Some panelists conduct 15/month; others had zero last quarter. No system distributes load systematically.
2
Assignment is entirely manual — TA has no load visibility
TA describe the assignment process as "coordinated manually" and "tedious and labour-intensive." No dashboard or tracker for who has conducted how many interviews.
3
High volume causes context-switching and cognitive load
26 of 31 panelists say volume "sometimes/often/always" affects core work focus. "Constant context-switching lowers overall cognitive efficiency" — a direct quote from the data.
Recommended actions
A
Build a simple interview load tracker
Track interviews conducted per panelist per month in a shared dashboard. TA uses this before assigning to ensure rotation. Even a spreadsheet solves the "who has done the most" problem.
B
Set a monthly interview cap per panelist
Define a ceiling (e.g. 8–10/month) based on role and seniority. Excess demand triggers panel expansion. HC has already flagged openness to training new interviewers.
C
Introduce fixed weekly interview slots
Majority of panelists prefer fixed slots over ad-hoc. TA publishes available slots weekly; panelists select preferred times. Reduces last-minute disruption to core project work.

Pain points
1
Last-minute candidate backouts with no early warning
"Most candidates accept and inform us at the last minute regarding backouts." No structured pre-joining engagement to identify at-risk candidates before they drop.
2
Seasonal patterns exist but aren't actioned
TA recognises declines peak around appraisal cycles and holidays. Candidates with longer notice periods are higher dropout risk. This insight exists in heads, not in data.
3
Mumbai location shows elevated drop-after-accept rate
Specifically flagged: "candidates are accepting offers but not joining" particularly for Mumbai positions. Location-specific retention issue that needs targeted action.
Recommended actions
A
Track acceptance vs decline by role and season
Start recording offer outcomes in a structured format: role, level, location, acceptance/decline, reason code. 3 months of data will reveal actionable patterns currently invisible.
B
Introduce a post-offer engagement touchpoint
A brief check-in call or message 1 week post-offer (especially for long-notice candidates) creates an early warning signal and builds engagement before joining.
C
Investigate Mumbai dropout pattern specifically
Surface the last 6 months of Mumbai offer data. Identify whether it's compensation, role, or competitor offers driving backouts. Consider a location-specific retention approach.

TA only
Pain points
1
No structured data on panel-level conversion rates
71% of TA say conversion ratios by panel don't exist or are just starting to be tracked. Without this, it's impossible to identify which panels are over/under-selecting.
2
Client-side delays extend the 15-day timeline
"Minimum 2 weeks, gets extended" due to client slot availability. Candidate interest drops during extended waits. Some strong candidates lost to competitor offers during this window.
3
Panel evaluation style varies — some seen as too strict
Recruiters observe variation: "some panels rigorous, others lenient." No standardised benchmark for what constitutes a pass at each level.
Recommended actions
A
Create a panel conversion dashboard
Track interview→offer ratios by panel, role, and skill set. Review quarterly. Flag panels with <30% or >80% conversion as outliers — both directions signal calibration issues.
B
Set an SLA for client feedback and offer timelines
Define a 10-business-day target from first panel interview to offer decision. Escalate to hiring manager when client slots cause delay beyond this threshold.
C
Publish pass criteria by level
Define minimum expectations for a "pass" at each role level. Reduces panel-to-panel variance and gives TA a benchmark to identify outlier panels.
